01 / Fix in this order

The six issues that matter most

Priority reflects the observed consequence and reviewer judgment. It is not a measured frequency or a usability score.

P1 · 01

The comparison understates missing exit evidence

A reader can leave with a more reassuring impression of both principal tokens than their own evidence supports.

Current State (Before)

“Executable exit” is Partial for both PTs. The matrix includes shared ONyc evidence but omits the PT liquidity records marked Missing.

Proposed State (After)

Separate underlying redemption evidence from position-specific execution. Add position-specific rows for PT exit, conversion, deployment and senior recovery. Link each state directly to its record.

Verified by comparing the matrix with the PT-specific evidence records. See screenshot 01 ↗

P1 · 02

Navigation lands in the middle of the new content

The destination appears without its heading or setup. The reader has to discover where the beginning went.

Current State (Before)

From the bottom of Overview, “Inspect exit routes” retains the previous scroll position: desktop lands around step 3; mobile lands at step 5.

Proposed State (After)

On a change of section, move the viewport and keyboard focus to the destination heading. Preserve scroll only when returning to the same content intentionally.

At 390 × 844, the route heading was 1,923 px above the viewport after the click. See screenshot 02 ↗

P1 · 03

A mobile layer selection changes content offscreen

The selected row updates, but the explanation stays out of view. The interaction does not bring its useful result to the reader.

Current State (Before)

The detail inspector sits below the full dependency stack. Selecting a layer changes that inspector without scrolling to it or moving focus.

Proposed State (After)

Expand the detail beside the selected row on mobile, or open an accessible detail panel. Keep the selected layer and its explanation visibly connected.

At 390 × 844, the inspector began 1,283 px below the viewport top after the click. See screenshot 03 ↗

P1 · 04

The page prioritizes structure over decision-critical risk

Readers must traverse a long dependency stack before reaching the factors that explain why approval is withheld.

Current State (Before)

The risk section starts at document y=1,709 on desktop and y=2,601 on mobile. PT-srONyc Overview shows 4 of 8 factors, omitting the underlying NAV contradiction.

Proposed State (After)

Place three material blockers directly under the assessment, including critical inherited risks. Add “Show all 8 risk factors” and keep the full dependency map available as deeper explanation.

Measured at 1440 × 900 and 390 × 844. The issue is reading order and selection of content, not whitespace alone.

P2 · 05

Mobile comparison loses the labels that make it comparable

When the reader swipes across, values remain visible while their row labels and the ONyc reference column disappear.

Current State (Before)

A 770 px table sits inside a 350 px visible window. Horizontal scrolling clips context; the far-right view leaves partial column content against the edge.

Proposed State (After)

Offer a two-position comparison on small screens, or keep a compact row-label column fixed with a clear scroll cue. Preserve the same fields in the same order.

Horizontal scrolling is supported, but task context is not preserved. See screenshot 04 ↗

P2 · 06

Applicability and the next research step are buried

A reader encounters a specific-looking assessment before finding the assumptions needed to decide whether it applies to their holding.

Current State (Before)

Scope follows 11 evidence cards at roughly y=3,245: September-series assumption, unspecified size, custody and eligibility. “Inspect scope & sources” opens the start of Evidence rather than the scope.

Proposed State (After)

Put a compact scope strip beside the result and three concrete approval blockers with direct links. Explain “NO_RATE” in plain English, then make the next research step clear.

Withholding unsupported grades is correct. The correction concerns visible scope and research guidance; it does not require an approval backend.

Three additional medium-priority issues

  • Unsaved notes disappear silently. Evidence → Overview → Evidence restores the saved note and discards the draft. Proposed: preserve drafts and clearly distinguish saved from unsaved work.
  • Source inspection stops short of inspectable claim support. The source modal does not expose the retained extract or exact API field; “inspect full record” has no actionable record link. Proposed: link each claim to its preserved field/value or document passage, and distinguish that support from analyst synthesis.
  • Dependency labels confuse the relationships. “PT-srONyc → Depends through PT-srONyc → wsrONyc” repeats internal notation; account exposures are called sibling branches, and D/S/R roles lack a nearby explanation. Proposed: name relationships and roles directly, and show junior protection attached to the senior claim.

03 / Readiness judgment

Keep the foundation. Repair the core tasks.

What is worth keeping

  • Clear separation of approved ratings from incomplete research.
  • Explicit dates, evidence states and unresolved dependencies.
  • A restrained Kurtosis palette and consistent visual language.
  • Detailed research that can support a better task flow.

What would change the judgment

First correct the comparison, the two interaction failures and the risk hierarchy. Then surface the applicable scope and the next research step.

Validate with intended analysts: identify the most important blocker, compare inherited and additional risks, trace a claim to evidence, and find the next required action. Observe completion, wrong interpretations and hesitation.

04 / Basis and limits

Expert inspection, not a user study

What was established

Browser walkthroughs, viewport measurements and inspection of the underlying records established the behaviors described above. The core flows were reviewed on desktop and a 390 × 844 mobile viewport.

The earlier 14 integrity tests and 16 browser tests, including automated accessibility checks, established useful functional coverage. They did not measure analyst comprehension, task success or real-user effort. No actual users participated in this audit.

How the critique was formed

Visibility of results, consistent summaries, preserved context and recognition over recall guide the interpretation of the observed failures. These are expert heuristics, not claims about how often users will fail. Nielsen Norman Group: usability heuristics ↗
